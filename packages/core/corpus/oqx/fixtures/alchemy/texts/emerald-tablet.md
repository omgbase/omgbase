# The Emerald Tablet

*Tabula Smaragdina.* A few hundred words attributed to Hermes Trismegistus,
earliest surviving in Arabic around the 8th century, and the most quoted text in
the entire tradition.

length:: short
attributed_to:: hermes

## The text

> That which is above is from that which is below, and that which is below is
> from that which is above, working the miracles of one.

> And as all things have been and arose from one by the mediation of one, so all
> things have their birth from this one thing by adaptation.

## Why it dominated

It is short enough to memorize, concrete enough to sound like instructions, and
vague enough to support any reading. Every practitioner from
[Jabir](/practitioners/jabir-ibn-hayyan.md) to [Newton](/practitioners/newton.md)
produced a commentary, and the commentaries agree on nothing.

Newton's translation survives in his own hand. He read it, plausibly, as
describing distillation and condensation — the below rising and the above
returning — which maps onto [dissolution](/processes/dissolution.md) and
[coagulation](/processes/coagulation.md).

## Assessment

Read as chemistry it says nothing operational. Read as a statement that the same
laws hold at every scale, it is a decent piece of natural philosophy that happens
to be true, arrived at for no good reason.
