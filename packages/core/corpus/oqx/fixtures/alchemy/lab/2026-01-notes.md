# Laboratory notes — January

Bench work on [calcination](/processes/calcination.md), chasing the weight gain.

operator:: bb
balance:: analytical

## Setup

Lead sheet cut into 2 g pieces, crucible pre-fired to constant weight, muffle at
600 °C. Weighings after cooling in a desiccator.

```text
tare crucible → weigh Pb → 600C 2h → cool in desiccator → reweigh
```

## Results

| Run | Pb in (g) | Calx out (g) | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2.001 | 2.140 | +0.139 |
| 2 | 2.004 | 2.147 | +0.143 |
| 3 | 1.998 | 2.138 | +0.140 |

Consistent gain of about 7% by mass. The colour went yellow then, on longer
heating, red — massicot to minium, as expected.

The gain is real and reproducible on a bench with 1830s-grade equipment. Anyone
doing this carefully before Lavoisier had the fact in hand; what was missing was
a framework that could accept it.

## Side work

Burned a batch of wormwood to ash and leached it while the muffle was up to
temperature: 40 g dried herb gave 1.1 g of crude salt after a single
recrystallization. That is the [[salt]] of the tria prima, obtained in an
afternoon, and it is easy to see why the operation carried evidential weight.

Sulphide colours are next — sulphur over the same metals, recording colour
against stoichiometry.
