# Laboratory notes — February

Recrystallization series on alum, testing the "purity gain per cycle" claim from
[coagulation](/processes/coagulation.md).

operator:: bb
balance:: analytical

## Method

Saturate at 80 °C, filter hot through a warmed funnel, cool undisturbed to room
temperature overnight, harvest, then re-saturate the mother liquor.

```text
saturate 80C → hot filter → cool overnight → harvest → repeat on liquor
```

## Results

| Cycle | Crop (g) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 14.2 | large clean octahedra |
| 2 | 5.8 | slightly smaller, still clear |
| 3 | 1.9 | small, some inclusions |
| 4 | 0.4 | poor, cloudy |

Yield falls off fast. Crystal quality is best in cycle 1 and degrades as
impurities concentrate in the liquor — the opposite of the alchemical expectation
that repeated cycling *improves* the product indefinitely.

The refinement is real but bounded, and it plateaus by cycle 3. That is exactly
the asymptote the tradition mistook for a trajectory.

## Conclusion

`solve et coagula` purifies. It does not transform. The claim in
[the philosophers' stone](/substances/philosophers-stone.md) does not follow from
this bench behaviour, and would not have followed for anyone who weighed the
crops.
