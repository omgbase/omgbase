# Paracelsus

Theophrastus von Hohenheim, 1493–1541. Redirected alchemy from metallurgy to
medicine, and was insufferable about it.

known_for:: tria prima
century:: 16

## The tria prima

He replaced the four elements with three principles — [[mercury]], [[sulphur]],
[[salt]] — as the constituents of every body. See [index](/index.md) for the
scheme.

As chemistry this is wrong. As a research programme it was an improvement: the
tria prima are *operationally defined* by what happens when you heat a substance
and see what flies off, what burns, and what remains. The four elements are not
defined by any operation at all.

## Iatrochemistry

His real contribution: minerals as medicines, prepared by chemical operation and
dosed by weight. Mercury for syphilis, antimony as an emetic, laudanum for pain.
Some of these worked. Several were poisons.

> All things are poison and nothing is without poison; only the dose makes a
> thing not a poison.

That is the founding statement of dose–response, and it is correct.

## Character

He burned Avicenna's books publicly, lectured in German rather than Latin,
insulted the faculty of every university that employed him, and was run out of
Basel. His posthumous reputation was built by followers who systematized what he
left as invective.
