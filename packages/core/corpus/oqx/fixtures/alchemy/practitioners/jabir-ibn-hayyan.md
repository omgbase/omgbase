# Jabir ibn Hayyan

8th–9th century, the towering figure of the Islamic alchemical tradition, and the
source — through the Latin *Geber* — of most of what medieval Europe knew.

known_for:: mercury-sulphur theory
century:: 8

## The mercury–sulphur theory

Every metal is a compound of [[mercury]] and [[sulphur]]; the proportion and the
purity of the mixture determine which metal you have. Gold is the balanced,
perfectly mixed case.

The consequence is the whole transmutation programme: if lead and gold differ
only in proportion, changing lead into gold requires no new substance, just
rebalancing. This is a *coherent* research question given the premises, and it
kept competent people busy for eight centuries.

## Method

More important than the theory is the insistence on procedure. The Jabirian
corpus specifies quantities, apparatus, and sequence, and expects the reader to
reproduce the result. It describes the preparation of what became the mineral
acids — see [dissolution](/processes/dissolution.md) — and classifies substances
by observable behaviour rather than by symbol.

That is recognisably experimental practice, several centuries before the
vocabulary existed for it.

## The corpus problem

Thousands of works bear the name; they cannot all be one author. The consensus is
a school writing under an authoritative name over generations. Attribution
questions remain open.
