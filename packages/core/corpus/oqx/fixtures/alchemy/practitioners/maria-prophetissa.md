# Maria Prophetissa

Alexandrian alchemist, probably 1st–3rd century, known only through quotation.
The earliest named practitioner whose *apparatus* survives in continuous use.

known_for:: balneum mariae
century:: 3

## The water bath

The *balneum Mariae* — Mary's bath — is a vessel heated indirectly by sitting it
in hot water. It holds a temperature that cannot exceed the boiling point of
water, which is exactly what you want for anything that scorches.

It is still standard equipment, still called a bain-marie, and it works for the
same reason it always did. Very little else in the alchemical inventory has that
record.

She is also credited with the *tribikos*, a three-armed still, and the *kerotakis*,
a sealed vessel for exposing metals to vapours.

## The axiom

> One becomes two, two becomes three, and out of the third comes the one as the
> fourth.

Quoted endlessly and explained never. Readings range from a distillation
schedule to a numerological gloss on the four stages of the
[magnum opus](/processes/magnum-opus.md). The honest position is that we do not
know what she meant.

## Assessment

Her reputation rests on hardware, not theory — and the hardware was right. Among
the practitioners here she is the one whose contribution needed no correction.
