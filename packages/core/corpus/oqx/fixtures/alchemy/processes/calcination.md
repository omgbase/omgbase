# Calcination

Heating a substance in air until it is reduced to a friable powder — a *calx*.
The first of the twelve gates and, in practice, the first thing anyone does.

element:: fire
reversible:: no

## Procedure

Grind the material, spread it thinly in an open crucible, and heat steadily
below the melting point. Stir to expose fresh surface. Continue until no further
weight is lost and no fumes come off.

```text
crucible → grind → thin layer → moderate heat → stir → constant weight
```

The endpoint is the constant weight, not the colour. Colour tells you which
oxide you have; weight tells you that you are finished.

## What is actually happening

Organic matter burns away; metals take up oxygen. The characteristic *gain* in
weight when calcining a metal is the observation that eventually broke the
phlogiston theory — the calx weighs more than the metal, which a theory of
escaping substance cannot accommodate.

Alchemical practice recorded this gain for centuries without drawing the
inference. The data were sound; the framework could not use them.

## Products

- calcined lead → yellow massicot, then red minium
- calcined copper → black tenorite
- plant matter → ash, source of the [[salt]]
