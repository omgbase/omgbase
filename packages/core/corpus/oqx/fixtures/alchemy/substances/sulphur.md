# Sulphur

Brimstone: yellow, brittle, and burns with a blue flame and a choking smell.
Its behaviour under heat made it the natural emblem of combustibility.

symbol:: 🜍
melting_point:: 115.2
toxic:: mildly

## As a principle

Sulphur is the soul — the combustible principle, what makes a substance
flammable and gives it colour and character. It mediates between the volatile
[[mercury]] and the fixed [[salt]].

In the mercury–sulphur theory inherited from
[Jabir ibn Hayyan](/practitioners/jabir-ibn-hayyan.md), every metal is a
compound of the two, and the proportions determine the metal. Gold is a perfect
balance; lead is the same pair badly mixed. Transmutation therefore requires no
new matter, only a correction of proportion — which is why the theory was so
durable and so encouraging.

## As a material

Sulphur combines directly with most metals on heating, producing sulphides whose
colours are dramatic and reproducible. This is genuinely useful chemistry, and
much of the practical content of the alchemical corpus concerns it. The bench
work is tracked in the [January notes](/lab/2026-01-notes.md).
